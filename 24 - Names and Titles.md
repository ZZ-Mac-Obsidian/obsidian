# Names and Titles

What slop games are called, why those names get clicked, and how to name a new one. Built from about 300 live titles read off Roblox's own chart lists (Top Playing Now, Top Trending, Up-and-Coming, Fun with Friends) and two trackers on 11 Nov 2026. The tile's picture is covered in [[17 - Icons Thumbnails and CTR]]; this note is the words.

Tags: (measured) read from the live charts, (official) Roblox docs, (my reading) interpretation, not tested data. Nobody outside Roblox has click-through data per title, so the "why" sections are reasoning from what the charts reward.

## The headline finding

**The name is the instruction.** Nearly every fast meme game on the charts is named with the exact thing you do, in three or four short words a seven-year-old can read:

> Steal An Egg · Throw Food For Animals · Grow a Garden · Net a Fish! · Cut a Rock · Build a Bridge · Ride A Pet · Sweep The Moon · Sell Fries! · Kick a Lucky Block · Peel a Potato · Feed A Mosquito

The player knows the whole game before clicking. That lowers the risk of the click, and it matches what Roblox measures: a clear promise that the game then keeps means a low first-minute bounce.

**The three biggest new hits carry no tags, no emoji and no brackets at all:** Steal An Egg (about 490,000 playing), Throw Food For Animals (about 164,000), Break and Steal an Egg (about 67,000) (measured). A strong plain name does not need decoration.

## The naming formulas

All examples are live chart titles (measured).

| Formula | Examples | What it promises |
|---|---|---|
| **Verb + a/an + Noun** | Steal An Egg, Steal a Brainrot, Grow a Garden, Net a Fish!, Cut a Rock, Raise A Catboy, Harpoon a Fish, Run a Motel, Merge a Mini Army, Roll a Fat Friend, Stack A Butter Squishy | one action on one funny thing |
| **Verb + Thing + For + Reward** | Throw Food For Animals, Escape Tsunami For Brainrots!, Hike For Eggs, Bike For Egg!, Climb For Animals Eggs, Cross Rivers for Animals | effort, then a prize |
| **+1 + Stat or Thing** | +1 Speed Keyboard Escape, +1 Venom Evolution, +1 Gun Forge, +1 Spider Arm, +1 Baddie Evolution, +1 Anime Eyes | you always get stronger |
| **Two verbs spliced** | Break and Steal an Egg, Steal A Pet And Ride It!, Detect and Steal an Egg, Build and Sell Houses!, Paint and Hide! | a known loop plus a twist, stated outright |
| **The dare** | DONT SPLASH, Do Not Stop., DONT SWIM, Find or Die!, Steal Or Die, Don't Miss The Rocket! | a rule you will want to break or beat |
| **The countdown or number** | 13 Days to Escape, 30 Floors Up, 100 Days at Sea, 7 Days Cat-Sitting, Sell a Million Sushi, Survive a Million Monkeys | a finish line, or an absurd scale |
| **The question** | Can You Pass A Driving Test? | a challenge aimed at the reader |
| **The lowercase sentence** | obby but you get smaller every stage, hide to survive, fight in a school, something evil will happen | reads like a friend describing it; stands out by looking unpolished |
| **Be or become** | Be a Soccer Legend, Become the Potato! | an identity |
| **Adjective + genre** | Hyper Realistic Ragdoll, Bloody Ragdoll Physics, Realistic Street Soccer | a quality claim on a known toy |
| **Thing + Simulator / Evolution / Incremental / RNG / Tycoon** | Tap Simulator REBORN, Barber Simulator, Bug Evolution, Depth Incremental, Fish an Anime RNG | a genre label older players search for |

Counts in the top 98 by players (measured): 4 titles contain "Steal", 5 contain "Simulator", 2 start with "+1" (4 more just outside), none contain "Tycoon". The verb-first forms are where the new hits are; "Simulator" and "Tycoon" are older language.

## Why these names get clicked (my reading)

1. **Zero effort to understand.** The whole pitch is read in under a second, at tile size, by a child. Abstract or invented names ("Azure Latch", "Vanguards") need a reputation first; verb names need none.
2. **A little mischief.** Steal, break, fake, throw, kick, slap, troll. The verbs that chart are things a child is not allowed to do at home. Harmless transgression is the single strongest flavour in the genre.
3. **Absurd specificity.** "Butter Squishy", "Fat Friend", "Mosquito", "Catboy", "Balut", "Baddie". The noun is concrete, slightly wrong and funny to say out loud. A specific silly noun beats a generic one every time: "Feed A Mosquito" over "Feed a Pet".
4. **A guaranteed feeling.** "+1" promises progress with no skill. "Million" promises scale. "Escape" promises tension. "Lucky" promises a jackpot. Each is a feeling the player can predict.
5. **A dare.** Titles phrased as an order or a question pull a reaction: "DONT SPLASH" makes the reader imagine splashing.
6. **Familiar plus one new thing.** "Steal an Egg" borrows the known "Steal a ..." frame and changes one word. The reader recognises the genre and sees the difference in the same glance. This is also the trap: change only the noun and the game is one of a hundred clones.
7. **It is how players talk.** These names are sentences children say to each other: "let's play steal an egg". A name that works as speech spreads by speech, and is what gets typed into search.
8. **It names the clip.** The title is also the caption of the 15-second video and the thumbnail. One idea, three places.

## Decoration: tags, emoji, case, punctuation

What the charts show (measured), and what Roblox says (official).

- **Front bracket tag** for freshness: `[UPD]`, `[UPD 21]`, `[UPDATE 2.0]`, `[NEW LAB]`, `[RELEASE!]`, `[HUGE UPDATE!]`. Very common on trending lists.
- **Emoji as a tag:** `[💀] Steal a Brainrot`, `[⚡] Steal a Superpower!`, `[🐟] Net a Fish!`. One emoji in brackets in front is the standard form; it adds a spot of colour to the text line.
- **Trailing emoji** matching the noun: `Sweep The Moon 🌕`, `Fisch 🦈`, `MOUSE 🐭`.
- **Seasonal marks:** most titles carried 🎃 during Halloween week.
- **Stage or genre labels:** `[ALPHA]`, `[BETA]`, `[HORROR]`, `[ASMR]`. Honest expectation-setting.
- **World or theme suffix after a bar:** `+1 Speed Keyboard Escape | Candy & Chocolate`. Changes with each world.
- **ALL CAPS** for dares and brands: `DONT SPLASH`, `RIVALS`, `DOORS`.
- **Exclamation mark** on playful ones: `Net a Fish!`, `Sell Fries!`.
- **Title Case** is the default; all-lowercase is a deliberate style.

Roblox's own naming advice is to avoid renaming often (players lose the game under its old name) and to avoid unrelated text such as version numbers or update notes in the name; titles that lead with money or giveaways get less exposure; irrelevant keywords and names resembling existing games lose priority (official). The charts are full of update tags anyway. A sensible middle:

- Keep the **core name fixed forever**.
- Use **at most one short tag in front**, changed only when a real update ships.
- Launch with **no tag**. The cleanest names on the chart are the biggest.
- Never put "free", "Robux", "codes" or another game's name in the title.

## Length

- Median on the top 98 is about 16 to 17 characters (estimate from the chart).
- Aim for **2 to 4 words, under about 22 characters** before any tag, so it is not cut off on a phone tile.
- Shorter words beat fewer words. "Cut a Rock" reads faster than "Excavation".

## Rules for a good name

1. **Starts with the verb** the player does every few seconds.
2. **Names one concrete, funny noun.**
3. **Contains the twist**, or implies it. If the twist is a second verb, splice it in.
4. **Readable by a seven-year-old**, and easy to say and spell for search.
5. **Works as a sentence between friends:** "want to play ___?"
6. **Matches the thumbnail and the first five seconds exactly.**
7. **Is not a one-word swap of a current hit** unless the game itself is genuinely different. Roblox favours whichever game first had the unique listing, and the original keeps nearly all the players.
8. **Is free:** search Roblox for it first.
9. **Uses no brand, show, or existing character name,** and nothing resembling "Roblox" or "Blox" in a new title (official).

## How to generate names

Fill the frames, then cut.

**Verbs that chart:** Steal, Break, Throw, Grow, Build, Ride, Raise, Feed, Cut, Dig, Sell, Run, Escape, Survive, Climb, Kick, Roll, Stack, Merge, Catch, Net, Fake, Hide, Launch, Sweep, Peel.

**Noun recipe:** something everyday + one odd adjective, or a creature crossed with an object. Food, animals, body parts, household things and internet slang all work. The funnier it is to say aloud, the better.

**Frames**

- `[Verb] a [Noun]`
- `[Verb] [Thing] For [Reward]`
- `+1 [Stat] [Noun or Verb]`
- `[Verb] and [Verb] a [Noun]`
- `DONT [Verb]` / `[Verb] or [Consequence]`
- `[Number] [Days or Floors] to [Verb]`
- `[genre] but [rule that changes it]`

**Method**

1. Write the twist sentence from [[20 - Slop Game Blueprints]].
2. Generate thirty names across at least four frames.
3. Strike any that need explaining, are hard to spell, or differ from a live hit by one word.
4. Say the survivors aloud. Keep the ones that make someone smile.
5. Search each on Roblox. Drop the taken ones.
6. Put the top three on mock tiles beside five real competitors at phone size. Pick the one read first.
7. Check it against the thumbnail idea: same verb, same noun, same twist.

## The description under the name

Roblox's guidance (official): the description should tell players and the discovery system what to expect; the limit is about 1,000 characters; hashtags and tag lists are not used in ranking; do not list controls.

A structure that fits both that and what chart games do:

1. One sentence saying what you do, using the title's verb.
2. Three to five short lines of how the loop goes, as verbs.
3. The latest update in two lines.
4. Group reward and where codes are posted.

Write it in natural language with the genre words a player would search ("steal", "pets", "tycoon", "obby") used once each, where they are true.

## Renaming and updates

- The core name does not change after launch.
- With each headline update: swap the front tag, or the suffix after the bar, and add a thumbnail. See [[22 - Live Ops and Updates]].
- A relaunch word ("REBORN", "2", "INFINITY") is for bringing back lapsed players of a known game, not for a new one.

## Name checklist

- [ ] Verb first, concrete funny noun, 2 to 4 words
- [ ] States or implies the twist
- [ ] A child can read, say and spell it
- [ ] Works in "want to play ___?"
- [ ] Not a one-word swap of a current hit
- [ ] Not taken; no brand, no "Robux", no "free"
- [ ] Same verb and noun as the thumbnail and the first five seconds
- [ ] No tag at launch; at most one afterwards
