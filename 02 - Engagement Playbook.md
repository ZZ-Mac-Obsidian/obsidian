# Engagement Playbook

What makes players stay in the first minutes and come back over four weeks, and exactly what Roblox measures. Tags: (official) Roblox docs read 11 Nov 2026, (study) peer-reviewed, (third-party) trackers and reports.

## What Roblox measures

The Home page's Recommended For You sort decides most of a game's traffic. Its signals (official):

**Most important**

| Signal | Meaning | Driven by |
|---|---|---|
| Play-through rate | share of people who see the tile and play | [[17 - Icons Thumbnails and CTR]] |
| First-play bounce (negative) | leaving in under 60 seconds, and in 61 to 180 seconds | loading time, the first five seconds, an honest thumbnail |
| Play days per user | unique days played in day 1, days 2 to 7, days 8 to 28 | return reasons below, [[22 - Live Ops and Updates]] |
| Playtime per user | time in game, **capped at 60 minutes per player per day** | core loop, session goals |

**Important**

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Intentional co-play days | days players return to play with friends by join, invite or private server |
| Qualified play sessions | meaningful sessions, filtering out accidental clicks and quick bounces |
| Spend days per user | unique days on which a player spends Robux |
| Robux spent per user | average spend |

How they are applied (official):

- **Averages per player, not totals.** A small game with engaged players competes with a large one.
- **Only players who found the game organically on Home count.** Players first acquired by ads, search, friends or social media are excluded from ranking signals. Ads do not hurt ranking; they just do not feed it.
- **New games do not wait 28 days.** Play-through and bounce count at once; day-1, day 2 to 7 and day 8 to 28 arrive in stages.
- **No penalty for a weak start.** Signals use a moving window, so a fixed game recovers.
- **No single signal decides ranking,** and genre is not a ranking factor.
- Games strong in both retention and monetization "might receive broader distribution".
- Finite and story games are not penalised for lower late retention.
- Near-duplicates of existing games, and listings that do not match the gameplay, lose priority.

**The shape this rewards:** an honest tile, a first minute that holds, a reason to return every day for a few minutes, friends, and a fair shop. Hours of grinding past the first hour of a day earn nothing.

## The first five seconds

- The player is already doing the core action. No menu, cutscene, long loading screen or pop-up.
- Spawn facing the thing to do, close enough to reach in two seconds.
- Keep the first area light so it loads fast on a phone; stream the rest.

## The first three minutes

- First reward by 30 seconds, first upgrade by 90, first big payoff by 3 minutes.
- Failure teaches the rule (the first fall, the first wave, the first theft) instead of a text box.
- Other players are the tutorial: put newcomers where they can see someone further along.
- Follow the timetable in [[01 - Game Standard]].

## Why the core action feels good

- **Instant visible result** and strong done-versus-undone contrast.
- **Small finishable chunks**, each with its own reward and name.
- **Completion** is the most stable motivation across ages (Quantic Foundry).
- **Getting closer speeds people up** (goal-gradient, study). Show percent and "2 left".
- **A head start raises completion** (endowed progress, study). Pre-tick the first starter goal.
- **People return to unfinished tasks** (study). Save mid-task and show what was left on return.
- **Unpredictable rewards hold attention** more than fixed ones. Use that in free rarity rolls and critical hits; see the cautions on paid randomness in [[03 - Monetization and Shop Standard]].
- **Moderate effects beat none and extreme** (study, N=3,018). Details in [[13 - Game Feel and Juice]].

## The session

- Sessions in current trend hits are short: about 9 minutes in the biggest +1 game, 15 to 16 in steal games (third-party). The median across Roblox is about 10.
- Give each session a natural end point: a rebirth, a full storage, a finished daily. A player who leaves satisfied returns; one who leaves bored does not.
- Keep three goals in view at once: one a minute away, one this session, one this week.

## Why players come back

| Scale | Reason | Build |
|---|---|---|
| Hours | something grew while away | offline earnings, capped ([[18 - AFK Idle and Offline Systems]]) |
| Hours | a public clock ticked | shop restock or rotation every few minutes, same for everyone |
| Daily | something new today | daily reward calendar, daily quest, playtime gifts |
| Daily | friends | friend boost, shared server boosts, free private servers |
| Weekly | new content on a fixed day | [[22 - Live Ops and Updates]] |
| Ongoing | something unfinished | collection index, rebirth ladder, leaderboards |
| Ongoing | something to show off | base, pets, overhead tag, auras visible to the server |

## Social hooks

Co-play days are a ranking signal, so playing with friends should be clearly better.

- A small income bonus per friend in the server.
- Server-wide boosts anyone can trigger.
- One-tap invite and join; free private servers.
- Things to do to each other that are funny rather than cruel: steal with counterplay, race, gift, and later trade.
- Server-wide announcements of rare finds, so one player's luck is everyone's event.

## What long-lived games share

- Weekly updates on a fixed day.
- Trading or social obligations between players.
- Additive content that never invalidates old progress.
- An identity of their own: name, characters, look.

## The line

| Do | Avoid | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Daily reward calendar that simply continues | Streaks that wipe progress on a missed day | Countdown timers that are inaccurate or restart for the same item (official) |
| Offline earnings with a visible cap | Progress that decays while away | Discounts that are always on, or false scarcity (official) |
| Real limited events that end when stated | Offers triggered by a loss | Thumbnails or titles that do not match the game (official) |
| Show progress and what is left | Near-miss staging on random rewards | Paid random items without full odds, or shown to restricted players (official) |
| AFK and auto features that pay less than playing | AFK income equal to active play | Faking input to dodge the idle disconnect (house rule) |
| Friends make it better | Loss with no counterplay | |
| Notifications opt-in, for facts | Titles that lead with money or giveaways (less exposure, official) | |
